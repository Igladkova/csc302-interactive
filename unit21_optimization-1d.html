This page summarizes Heath §6.4, CSc 302 meeting 21. It uses the optimality conditions of meeting 20 in one variable and mirrors the root finders of meetings 17–18. One-dimensional minimization is also the line-search subproblem inside the methods of meeting 22. Lab 11 practises it.

1. Unimodality and bracketing (Heath §6.4)

\(f\) is unimodal on \([a, b]\) if it has a single minimizer \(x^*\) there, strictly decreasing to the left of \(x^*\) and strictly increasing to its right. Then comparing \(f\) at two interior points \(x_1 < x_2\) tells us which end to discard: if \(f(x_1) > f(x_2)\) the minimum is not in \([a, x_1)\); otherwise it is not in \((x_2, b]\).

  • Unlike a sign change, unimodality cannot be checked from a few values. If \(f\) has several local minima in \([a, b]\), the search still converges, but possibly to a local minimum that is not the global one. Convex \(f\) are unimodal.
  • As at any minimum, the minimizer is determined only to about \(\sqrt{\epsilon_{\text{mach}}}\) (meeting 20, §4), so tolerances below about \(10^{-8}\) relative are wasted.

2. Golden section search (Heath §6.4.1)

Place the two interior points at fractions \(1 - \tau\) and \(\tau\) of the interval: \[ x_1 = a + (1 - \tau)(b - a), \qquad x_2 = a + \tau(b - a), \qquad \tau = \frac{\sqrt5 - 1}{2} \approx 0.618 . \] Because \(\tau^2 = 1 - \tau\), the surviving interior point is exactly at the right fraction of the new interval, so each step needs only one new function value. The interval shrinks by the factor \(\tau\) every step: linear convergence with \(C \approx 0.618\), slower than bisection's \(\tfrac12\). To reduce \([a, b]\) below a tolerance takes \[ k = \left\lceil \frac{\ln\bigl(\text{tol}/(b - a)\bigr)}{\ln \tau} \right\rceil \] steps. It uses only comparisons of \(f\) values: no derivatives, safe for any unimodal \(f\).

Examples

Example 1. Minimize \(f(x) = e^x - 2x\) on \([0, 2]\) (exact minimizer \(\ln 2 = 0.693147\), \(f^* = 0.613706\)). Do two steps of golden section search, and count the steps needed for an interval shorter than \(10^{-4}\).

Answer

\(x_1 = 0.7639\), \(x_2 = 1.2361\); \(f(x_1) = 0.6188 < f(x_2) = 0.9699\), so keep \([0, 1.2361]\). The old \(x_1\) becomes the new \(x_2\), and the new \(x_1 = 0.4721\); \(f(0.4721) = 0.6589 > f(0.7639)\), so keep \([0.4721, 1.2361]\). Steps for \(10^{-4}\): \(\lceil \ln(5\times10^{-5}) / \ln 0.618 \rceil = 21\) (bisection on \(f'\) would need 15, but needs derivatives).

Example 2. \(g(x) = \sin 3x + x/2\) has local minima at \(x \approx 1.515\) (\(g = -0.229\), the global minimum) and \(x \approx 3.609\) (\(g = 0.819\)). Where does golden section search on \([1, 5]\) end up?

Answer

The first points are \(2.528\) (\(g = 2.228\)) and \(3.472\) (\(g = 0.899\)). Since \(g(x_1) > g(x_2)\), the search discards \([1, 2.528]\), which contains the global minimum, and converges to the local minimum 3.609. \(g\) is not unimodal on \([1, 5]\), so the comparison rule was not justified.

3. Successive parabolic interpolation (Heath §6.4.2)

Fit a parabola through the last three points \((u, f(u)), (v, f(v)), (w, f(w))\) and take its vertex as the next point, dropping the oldest point. The vertex is \(v - p/q\) with \[ p = (v - u)^2\,[f(v) - f(w)] - (v - w)^2\,[f(v) - f(u)], \qquad q = 2\bigl[(v - u)[f(v) - f(w)] - (v - w)[f(v) - f(u)]\bigr]. \] It uses the function values, not only their order, and converges superlinearly with \(r \approx 1.324\) near a minimum where \(f'' > 0\). Like the secant method, it is not guaranteed to converge from far away.

Example

Example 3. Successive parabolic interpolation for \(e^x - 2x\) from \(u, v, w = 0, 1, 2\).

Answer

Vertices: 0.5954, 0.7298, 0.6810, 0.69267, 0.693071, 0.6931483. Errors 0.098, 0.037, 0.012, \(4.8\times10^{-4}\), \(7.6\times10^{-5}\), \(1.1\times10^{-6}\): faster than golden section after a few steps, slower than Newton.

4. Newton's method for minimization (Heath §6.4.3)

Use the quadratic Taylor model \(f(x + h) \approx f(x) + f'(x)h + \tfrac12 f''(x)h^2\) and jump to its minimum, \(h = -f'(x)/f''(x)\): \[ x_{k+1} = x_k - \frac{f'(x_k)}{f''(x_k)} . \] This is Newton's method (meeting 18) applied to \(f'(x) = 0\): quadratic convergence near a minimizer with \(f''(x^*) > 0\), but it needs first and second derivatives and a good start. It finds any critical point (a maximum too, if \(f'' < 0\) there), so check \(f''(x_k) > 0\) or safeguard.

Example

Example 4. Newton's method for \(e^x - 2x\) from \(x_0 = 1\).

Answer

\(f' = e^x - 2\), \(f'' = e^x\), so \(x_{k+1} = x_k - 1 + 2e^{-x_k}\): 1, 0.735759, 0.694042, 0.6931476, 0.69314718. Errors 0.31, 0.043, \(9\times10^{-4}\), \(4\times10^{-7}\), \(8\times10^{-14}\): quadratic.

5. Comparison; safeguarded methods (Heath §6.4.4)

MethodNeedsRateSafety
Golden section\(f\) values (1 per step), a unimodal bracketlinear, \(C = 0.618\)always converges (to a local minimum)
Parabolic interpolation\(f\) values (1 per step), 3 starting pointssuperlinear, \(r \approx 1.324\)local only
Newton\(f'\) and \(f''\) per stepquadraticlocal only; may find a maximum

A safeguarded method combines speed and safety: keep a unimodal bracket, try a parabolic-interpolation step, and fall back on golden section when that step is not acceptable. Brent's method does this, and it is what scipy.optimize.minimize_scalar(method="bounded") (and MATLAB's fminbnd) use. For Example 1 it returns \(0.6931473\) after 9 function evaluations. The error \(7\times10^{-8}\) is consistent with the \(\sqrt{\epsilon}\)-level default tolerance.

7. Try it, and review questions

Interactive: Golden section search, Successive parabolic interpolation and Newton's method (1-D optimization). Lab 11 compares them.

  1. Define unimodal. Why can't you verify unimodality from a few function values, while a sign change is easy to check?
  2. Why are golden section points placed at fractions \(0.382\) and \(0.618\)? What goes wrong if you use \(\tfrac13\) and \(\tfrac23\)?
  3. How many golden section steps reduce an interval of length 1 below \(10^{-6}\)? How many function evaluations is that?
  4. Why is golden section slower per step than bisection on \(f'\), and when is it still the better choice?
  5. Write the parabolic interpolation step. What could make \(q = 0\)?
  6. Derive Newton's method for minimization from the quadratic Taylor model. Why may it converge to a maximum?
  7. Why is it pointless to ask a 1-D minimizer for \(|x - x^*| < 10^{-12}\) when it uses only \(f\) values?
  8. What does a safeguarded method such as Brent's guarantee?
