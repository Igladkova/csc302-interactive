Learning objectives

  • Explain why many low-degree pieces beat one high-degree polynomial, and bound the error of piecewise linear interpolation.

  • Compute a natural cubic spline through three points by hand.

  • Draw a curve through points in the plane by interpolating \(x(t)\) and \(y(t)\) separately, and compute its length.

1. Piecewise polynomial interpolation (Heath §7.4)

Use a different low-degree polynomial on each subinterval \([t_i, t_{i+1}]\) between knots \(t_1 < \cdots < t_n\). The simplest choice joins the data by straight lines. With spacing \(h\) its error is \[ |f(t) - \ell(t)| \le \frac{h^2}{8} \max |f''| , \] the meeting 24 error formula with two nodes on each piece. Halving \(h\) divides the error by about 4 however many points there are, so no Runge phenomenon. The price is smoothness: \(\ell\) has corners at the knots. The extra freedom of higher-degree pieces buys smoothness back.

Example

Example 1. Interpolate \(f(t) = \ln t\) on \([1, 2]\) by straight lines on 4 equal pieces. Bound the error and compare with the actual error; what happens with 8 and 16 pieces?

Answer

\(h = 0.25\) and \(|f''| = 1/t^2 \le 1\), so the error is at most \(0.25^2/8 = 7.8\times10^{-3}\). The actual maximum error is \(6.2\times10^{-3}\) (largest on the first piece, where \(f''\) is largest). With 8 and 16 pieces: \(1.7\times10^{-3}\) and \(4.6\times10^{-4}\), down by a factor of about 4 each time: \(O(h^2)\).

2. Hermite cubics and cubic splines (Heath §7.4.1–7.4.2)

With \(n\) knots there are \(n - 1\) cubic pieces and \(4(n - 1)\) coefficients. Interpolating the data at both ends of every piece gives \(2(n - 1)\) equations.

  • Hermite cubic (\(C^1\)): also match first derivatives at the \(n - 2\) interior knots, which leaves \(n\) free parameters. Fix them by prescribing the slope at every knot, either from known derivatives or from estimates out of the data. With slopes chosen to respect the data's shape (scipy.interpolate.PchipInterpolator), monotone data give a monotone interpolant.
  • Cubic spline (\(C^2\)): match first and second derivatives at the interior knots, \(2(n - 2)\) more equations, for \(4n - 6\) in all. Two conditions are still free, one at each end:
    • natural: \(s'' = 0\) at \(t_1\) and \(t_n\);
    • clamped: given end slopes;
    • not-a-knot: the same cubic on the first two and on the last two pieces (SciPy's default);
    • periodic: \(s'\) and \(s''\) agree at the two ends, for closed curves.

Written in a local or B-spline basis (Heath §7.4.3), the spline system is tridiagonal (banded), so it is solved in \(O(n)\) operations (meeting 10). For smooth \(f\) the cubic spline error is \(O(h^4)\).

Examples

Example 2. Find the natural cubic spline through \((-1, 1)\), \((0, 0)\), \((1, 3)\), and evaluate it at \(t = \pm\tfrac12\).

Answer

Let \(M = s''(0)\); the natural conditions give \(s''(\pm1) = 0\). For equal spacing \(h\), continuity of \(s'\) at the middle knot gives \(\tfrac{2h}{3} M = \frac{y_3 - y_2}{h} - \frac{y_2 - y_1}{h} = 3 - (-1) = 4\), so \(M = 6\). The pieces are \[ s(t) = 1 - 2(t + 1) + (t + 1)^3 \ \text{on } [-1, 0], \qquad s(t) = t + 3t^2 - t^3 \ \text{on } [0, 1]. \] Check: both give \(s(0) = 0\), \(s'(0) = 1\), \(s''(0) = 6\), and \(s(1) = 3\). Then \(s(-\tfrac12) = 0.125\) and \(s(\tfrac12) = 1.125\). (The 8×8 monomial system of Heath Example 7.6 gives the same spline.)

Example 3. The data \((0, 0), (1, 0), (2, 0), (3, 1), (4, 1)\) rise like a step. Compare the natural cubic spline with the monotone Hermite cubic (PCHIP).

Answer

The spline dips to \(-0.107\) near \(t = 1.62\) and overshoots to \(1.131\) near \(t = 3.42\): being \(C^2\), it cannot turn sharply. PCHIP stays within \([0, 1]\) and is monotone, at the cost of a jump in \(s''\) at the knots. If the values are concentrations or probabilities, the overshoot is physically wrong, so choose PCHIP. If smoothness matters most (a cam profile, a camera path), choose the spline.

3. Parametric curves through points (Stewart §10.1, §13.1–13.2)

A curve in the plane that loops or turns back is not a graph \(y = f(x)\). Write it as \(\mathbf r(t) = \langle x(t), y(t)\rangle\) and interpolate each coordinate as a function of a parameter. Use \(t = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\) at the points, or the cumulative chord length for unevenly spaced points. Then:

  • the tangent vector is \(\mathbf r'(t) = \langle x'(t), y'(t)\rangle\), which a cubic spline gives continuously (Stewart §13.2);
  • the length is \(L = \int \|\mathbf r'(t)\|\,dt = \int \sqrt{x'(t)^2 + y'(t)^2}\,dt\), usually computed by quadrature (Stewart §13.3, meeting 26);
  • for a closed curve, use periodic end conditions so the curve closes smoothly.

Example

Example 4. Fit periodic cubic splines \(x(t)\), \(y(t)\) to the 4 points \((1, 0), (0, 1), (-1, 0), (0, -1)\) on the unit circle (with \(t = 0, 1, 2, 3\) and back to the start at \(t = 4\)). How close is the length to \(2\pi\)? What about 8 and 16 points?

Answer

The spline curve has length \(6.1955\), versus \(2\pi = 6.2832\) (1.4% short); its distance from the origin dips to \(0.972\) between the data points. With 8 and 16 equally spaced points the length errors are \(-3.8\times10^{-3}\) and \(-2.1\times10^{-4}\). The error drops by a factor of about 18 per doubling, close to the \(2^4 = 16\) of an \(O(h^4)\) method.

6. Try it, and review questions

Interactive: B-splines, Bernstein polynomials and the Interpolation playground; also the Interpolation Playground (lecture version). Lab 12 draws spline curves through points and measures their length.

  1. Why does piecewise interpolation avoid Runge's phenomenon? What does it give up?
  2. For \(n\) knots, count the parameters and the conditions for a Hermite cubic and for a cubic spline.
  3. List four end conditions for a cubic spline. Which one would you use for a closed curve?
  4. How many pieces does piecewise linear interpolation of \(\sin t\) on \([0, \pi]\) need for error below \(10^{-4}\)?
  5. Why is the spline system tridiagonal in a local basis, and why does that matter?
  6. Give an application where a spline's overshoot is unacceptable and PCHIP is the better choice.
  7. Why interpolate \(x(t)\) and \(y(t)\) separately instead of \(y\) as a function of \(x\)?
  8. How would you compute the length of a spline curve, and how fast should the length converge as points are added?
