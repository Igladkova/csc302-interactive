Review: double integrals and change of variables

Review · Stewart §15.1, §15.3, §15.4, §15.9 (2-D)

Learning objectives

  • Interpret \(\iint_R f\,dA\) as a limit of double Riemann sums, and estimate it with the Midpoint Rule.
  • Evaluate double integrals over rectangles as iterated integrals (Fubini's Theorem), choosing a convenient order.
  • Compute mass, center of mass and probabilities with double integrals.
  • Change variables in a double integral using the Jacobian determinant, with polar coordinates as the main case.

This page summarizes Stewart §15.1, §15.3, §15.4 and §15.9 (two dimensions only).

1. Double integrals over rectangles (Stewart §15.1)

Divide \(R = [a, b] \times [c, d]\) into \(m \times n\) subrectangles \(R_{ij}\) of area \(\Delta A\), and pick a sample point \((x_{ij}^*, y_{ij}^*)\) in each. The double integral is the limit of the double Riemann sums (Stewart, Definition 15.1.5): \[ \iint_R f(x, y)\,dA = \lim_{m, n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n} f(x_{ij}^*, y_{ij}^*)\,\Delta A . \] If \(f \ge 0\), it is the volume under the surface \(z = f(x, y)\) above \(R\).

  • Midpoint Rule: take the centers \((\bar x_i, \bar y_j)\) as sample points: \(\iint_R f\,dA \approx \sum_i\sum_j f(\bar x_i, \bar y_j)\,\Delta A\).
  • Fubini's Theorem (Stewart, Theorem 15.1.10): for continuous \(f\) on \(R\), \[ \iint_R f\,dA = \int_a^b\!\!\int_c^d f(x, y)\,dy\,dx = \int_c^d\!\!\int_a^b f(x, y)\,dx\,dy . \] Integrate the inner integral treating the other variable as a constant. Pick the order that makes the inner integral easier.
  • If \(f(x, y) = g(x)h(y)\), the integral factors: \(\bigl(\int_a^b g\bigr)\bigl(\int_c^d h\bigr)\).

Examples

Example 1. Estimate \(\iint_R xy^2\,dA\), \(R = [0, 2] \times [0, 1]\), with the Midpoint Rule, \(m = n = 2\). Compare with the exact value.

Answer

Midpoints \(x = 0.5, 1.5\) and \(y = 0.25, 0.75\); \(\Delta A = 1 \times 0.5 = 0.5\). Sum: \(0.5\,(0.03125 + 0.28125 + 0.09375 + 0.84375) = 0.625\). Exact: \(\int_0^2 x\,dx \int_0^1 y^2\,dy = 2 \cdot \tfrac13 = 0.6667\).

Example 2. Evaluate \(\iint_R x e^{xy}\,dA\), \(R = [0, 1] \times [0, 1]\). Which order is easier?

Answer

Integrate in \(y\) first: \(\int_0^1 x e^{xy}\,dy = \bigl[e^{xy}\bigr]_{y=0}^{1} = e^x - 1\). Then \(\int_0^1 (e^x - 1)\,dx = e - 2 \approx 0.7183\). Integrating in \(x\) first would need integration by parts.

2. Applications (Stewart §15.4)

For a thin plate (lamina) occupying \(D\) with density \(\rho(x, y)\): \[ m = \iint_D \rho\,dA, \qquad \bar x = \frac1m \iint_D x\rho\,dA, \qquad \bar y = \frac1m \iint_D y\rho\,dA . \] With \(\rho\) constant, \((\bar x, \bar y)\) is the centroid of \(D\). For random variables \(X, Y\) with joint density \(f\) (\(f \ge 0\), \(\iint_{\mathbb R^2} f = 1\)), \(P\bigl((X, Y) \in D\bigr) = \iint_D f\,dA\), and \(E[X] = \iint x f\,dA\).

Example

Example 3. \(X\) and \(Y\) have joint density \(f(x, y) = 6e^{-2x - 3y}\) for \(x, y \ge 0\) (0 otherwise). Check that \(f\) is a density, and find \(P(X \le 1, Y \le 1)\).

Answer

\(\int_0^\infty\!\int_0^\infty 6e^{-2x}e^{-3y}\,dy\,dx = 6 \cdot \tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac13 = 1\). \(P = \int_0^1 2e^{-2x}dx \int_0^1 3e^{-3y}dy = (1 - e^{-2})(1 - e^{-3}) \approx 0.8216\).

3. Change of variables; polar coordinates (Stewart §15.9 in 2-D; polar coordinates from §15.3)

A transformation \(T(u, v) = (x(u, v), y(u, v))\) stretches a small rectangle \(\Delta u \times \Delta v\) into a small near-parallelogram of area \(\approx |J|\,\Delta u\,\Delta v\), where the Jacobian (Stewart, Definition 15.9.7) is \[ J = \frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(u, v)} = \det\begin{bmatrix} x_u & x_v \\ y_u & y_v \end{bmatrix}. \] Hence (Stewart, Theorem 15.9.9), if \(T\) maps \(S\) one-to-one onto \(D\), \[ \iint_D f(x, y)\,dA = \iint_S f\bigl(x(u, v), y(u, v)\bigr)\,\Bigl|\frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(u, v)}\Bigr|\,du\,dv . \]

  • Polar coordinates: \(x = r\cos\theta\), \(y = r\sin\theta\) has \(J = r\), so \(dA = r\,dr\,d\theta\) (Stewart, Formula 15.3.2; CSc 302 treats polar coordinates as the main example of §15.9). Use it for disks, annuli, sectors, and integrands containing \(x^2 + y^2\).
  • Linear maps \(x = au + bv\), \(y = cu + dv\) have constant \(|J| = |ad - bc|\). They map parallelograms to rectangles and ellipses to circles.
  • Never forget the factor \(|J|\): without it the answer is wrong.

Examples

Example 4. Evaluate \(\iint_D \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\,dA\), where \(D\) is the upper half of the annulus \(1 \le x^2 + y^2 \le 4\).

Answer

In polar coordinates \(D\) is \(1 \le r \le 2\), \(0 \le \theta \le \pi\), and the integrand is \(r\): \(\int_0^\pi\!\int_1^2 r \cdot r\,dr\,d\theta = \pi \cdot \frac{8 - 1}{3} = \frac{7\pi}{3} \approx 7.330\).

Example 5. Find the centroid of the quarter disk \(x^2 + y^2 \le 1\), \(x, y \ge 0\).

Answer

Area \(\pi/4\). \(\iint x\,dA = \int_0^{\pi/2}\!\int_0^1 (r\cos\theta)\,r\,dr\,d\theta = \tfrac13\). So \(\bar x = \frac{1/3}{\pi/4} = \frac{4}{3\pi} \approx 0.4244\), and \(\bar y = \bar x\) by symmetry.

Example 6. Use \(x = 2u\), \(y = 3v\) to evaluate \(\iint_E x^2\,dA\) over the ellipse \(E: \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{9} \le 1\).

Answer

\(T\) maps the unit disk \(u^2 + v^2 \le 1\) onto \(E\), with \(J = \det\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = 6\). So \(\iint_E x^2\,dA = \iint_{u^2+v^2\le1} 4u^2 \cdot 6\,du\,dv = 24 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4} = 6\pi\), using \(\iint_{\text{disk}} u^2 = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^1 r^2\cos^2\theta\, r\,dr\,d\theta = \pi/4\).

4. Why CSc 302 needs this

  • Tensor-product quadrature: applying a 1-D rule (midpoint, trapezoid, Simpson, Gauss) in each direction is Fubini's Theorem done numerically. The cost grows like (points per direction)², which is why Monte Carlo wins in high dimensions.
  • The Jacobian determinant here and the Jacobian matrix of Newton's method are the same matrix: one measures area stretching, the other gives the linear model.
  • Probabilities and expected values are double integrals, and often only numerical ones.

Review questions

  1. Define \(\iint_R f\,dA\) as a limit. What does it represent when \(f \ge 0\)?
  2. Write the Midpoint Rule for \(m \times n\) subrectangles. How does its error behave as \(m = n\) doubles, for smooth \(f\)?
  3. State Fubini's Theorem. When does the integral factor into a product of single integrals?
  4. Write the formulas for mass and center of mass of a lamina.
  5. What conditions must a joint density satisfy? How do you compute \(P(X + Y \le 1)\)?
  6. Why is \(dA = r\,dr\,d\theta\) in polar coordinates? Show it with a picture of a polar rectangle.
  7. Write the change-of-variables formula. What is the Jacobian of \(x = u + v\), \(y = u - v\)?
  8. Evaluate \(\iint_{x^2 + y^2 \le a^2} e^{-(x^2 + y^2)}\,dA\), and let \(a \to \infty\).
